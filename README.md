# Śakti Mandala

Build an Immersive Animated Website:

ŚAKTI — The Living Universe

Create a visually extraordinary, deeply interactive educational website exploring Shaktism (Śāktism / Śākta traditions)— its philosophy, theology, goddesses, texts, Tantra, cosmology, sacred geography, practices, symbols, philosophical schools, history, and contemporary traditions.

This should not feel like a conventional religion website.

It should feel like entering a living cosmology of Śakti.

The central experience is:

Enter → Manifestation → Goddess → Cosmos → Tantra → Consciousness → Dissolution → Return

The website should combine:

Śākta sacred art

Indian temple architecture

yantras

mandalas

cosmic imagery

Himalayan landscapes

Bengal’s Śākta traditions

Kashmir Śaiva-Śākta philosophy

South Indian Śrīvidyā

subtle Tantric symbolism

Sanskrit manuscript aesthetics

particle systems

cinematic environmental animation

museum-quality educational design

restrained psychedelic/surreal imagery

The result should feel simultaneously ancient, cosmic, philosophical and futuristic.

Treat traditions respectfully and distinguish historical scholarship, scripture, philosophy, devotional belief, ritual tradition and modern interpretation.

2. The Central Idea

Introduce the question:

What Is Śakti?

Create an enormous field of moving particles.

The particles continuously transform between:

Energy → Matter → Life → Mind → Form → Dissolution

Explain that śakti broadly means:

power, capacity, potency or energy.

Within Śākta traditions, Śakti becomes much more than an abstract physical concept.

Explore ideas including:

divine power

cosmic manifestation

Goddess

consciousness

creation

transformation

Avoid reducing Śakti simply to the modern scientific concept of “energy.”

Provide a subtle educational note explaining that religious/metaphysical concepts of Śakti and scientific energy are not identical.

48. Historical and Intellectual Integrity

The website must continuously distinguish between:

Scripture

Theology

Philosophy

Ritual tradition

Devotional interpretation

Historical scholarship

Modern interpretation

Do not present every claim as though all Śāktas, Hindus or scholars agree.
